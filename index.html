/// On the name

DYSKEI, denotatively and connotatively.

DYSKEI was assembled from the initials of all six of us. We discovered, only after the letters were already arranged, that the word evokes I like you, very much in Korean. The coincidence felt earned. Read denotatively it is a six-letter team acronym. Read connotatively, it is the quiet sentiment six students chose for the professor who taught us how to see visual rhetoric and information technology with a critical eye. Dr. Pennings has spent this semester teaching us that meaning is always layered. DYSKEI is our small return of the favor.

A second reading sits behind the first. Dr. Pennings has spoken often of his Japanese-American wife, of his home country, and of the company in Austin, Texas, where he once worked. DYSKEI carries those threads in the same syllable. The team logo, a hexagonal emblem in royal blue and chrome silver, signals the technology we study, while the warmth of the word it spells signals the people who taught us to read it.
